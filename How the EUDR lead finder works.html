How the EUDR lead finder works

The skill finds new sales contacts for our EUDR solution (software that helps companies prove their products did not cause deforestation). It searches Apollo, checks HubSpot so nobody is contacted twice too soon, and delivers a ranked CSV file. Each numbered step below does one job, and the finished file is at the bottom.

Works on its own Asks Apollo Asks HubSpot

Start: one short sentence goes in

You write a request such as "20 EUDR leads for Germany" or "find EUDR rhinos in Belgium". The word EUDR and a place are needed. The skill starts automatically.

Number of contacts

How many leads you want, or an animal that implies it. If left out, the skill asks.

Place

A country or a region such as DACH or BENELUX.

Company size (optional)

Whale, Elephant, Rhino, Bear or Fox. Without it, all sizes from Fox to Elephant are searched.

Connections it uses

Apollo (contact database) and HubSpot (our CRM). Neither is changed. They are only read.

1

Understand your request

Works on its own

Picks three facts out of your message: how many contacts you want, where to look, and (optionally) how big the companies should be. If the number is missing, it asks you before doing anything else.

Result: a clear search brief: number, location and company size.

1a. How many contacts

Said directly ("20 leads") or implied by the size animal. If it is missing, the skill asks "How many contacts would you like?" and waits.

1b. Where to look

A country or a region. DACH means Germany, Austria and Switzerland. BENELUX means Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. If missing, the skill asks which country or region.

1c. Company size, named after animals optional

Taken from the osapiens Sales Playbook. Whale: over 25,000 employees and over $10bn revenue. Elephant: 10,000 to 25,000 employees or $5bn to $10bn. Rhino: 3,000 to 10,000 or $2bn to $5bn. Bear: 750 to 3,000 or $500m to $2bn. Fox: under 750 or under $500m.

No animal named

The skill searches the whole range from Fox to Elephant. Whales are only searched when asked for, because they must meet both size conditions.

2

Search for matching people

Asks Apollo

Asks Apollo, our contact database, for people who could buy the osapiens EUDR solution (the EU rules on deforestation-free products). It looks for decision makers in the right departments at companies that are affected by the rules.

Result: a first list of contacts, up to the number you asked for.

2a. Who counts as a decision maker

Seniority from manager up to the top: Manager, Director, Head of Department, VP, C-level.

2b. In which teams

Sustainability, Supply Chain, Procurement, Compliance, Legal, ESG, Regulatory Affairs and Operations.

2c. At which companies

Industries such as Retail, Food & Beverage, Manufacturing, Chemicals, Automotive, Paper & Packaging, Furniture, Consumer Goods and Logistics. Best fits sell into the EU and deal with goods such as coffee, cocoa, wood, paper, rubber or products made from them.

Companies that are left out

No link to the EU market, no affected goods, pure software or service companies, or very small firms with no legal duty and no customer pressure.

2d. If something goes wrong

Fewer people found than asked: the skill tells you how many exist and asks whether to continue. None found: it says so and waits. Apollo error: it shows the message "Upgrade Apollo API".

3

Check who we already know

Asks HubSpot

Looks up every contact in HubSpot, our CRM, so that we do not contact the same person twice in a short time.

Result: the column ENROLLED IN HS, and a shorter list.

3a. Search

Each person is searched by email, and by full name together with the company website.

3b. Sort into two groups

Not enrolled: not in HubSpot at all. Enrolled: already in HubSpot, and the date we last contacted them is noted.

3c. Remove recent contacts

Anyone contacted within the last 30 days is removed from the list completely.

3d. If HubSpot is not reachable

The skill shows "HubSpot is not well connected".

4

Put the best leads first

Works on its own

Sorts the remaining people so the freshest opportunities are at the top.

Result: an ordered list: new people first, then known people.

4a. Top of the list

Everyone who is not in HubSpot (brand-new leads).

4b. Bottom of the list

People already in HubSpot. The ones we contacted longest ago come first, the most recently contacted come last.

5

Fill in the contact details

Asks Apollo

Sends the shortened list back to Apollo to complete each person's profile.

Result: a complete row of details for every person.

5a. What is filled in

Email, city and country, seniority, job title, company website and LinkedIn profile link.

Nothing available

The cell stays empty. The skill never makes up a detail.

6

Create the CSV file

Works on its own

Writes everything into a spreadsheet-ready CSV file with 12 columns in a fixed order, and hands it to you as a download.

Result: one CSV file, ready for outreach.

6a. The 12 columns

First Name, Last Name, Job Title, Seniority, Department, Email, LinkedIn URL, Company Name, Company Domain, Location, ENROLLED IN HS, Last Contacted Date.

6b. File name

Built from the request, for example eudr-20-germany-2026-05-26.csv (number, place, date).

6c. Where it is saved

In the folder C:\Claude Skills\Claude Test EUDR ICP\, and also shown in the chat as a download.

7

Send a short summary

Works on its own

Finishes with a few lines in the chat so you see the outcome at a glance.

Result: a summary of what was found, removed and delivered.

7a. What the summary says

Region searched, total contacts found, how many were removed (contacted in the last 30 days), how many are brand-new, how many are known but cold, and the file name.

End

Output: the ranked lead list (CSV file)

You get one CSV file with up to the number of contacts you asked for, plus a short summary in the chat. New people are at the top, so you start with the freshest opportunities.
