How the verified email finder works

The skill finds a person's real work email address. Instead of guessing and hoping, it builds the likely addresses and tests each one with the company's mail server until one is confirmed. Each numbered step below does one job, and the finished answer is at the bottom.

Works on its own Runs a script Asks the mail checker

Start: a name and a company go in

You ask something like "What is Anna Beispiel's email at Acme?" or hand over a whole list. Nothing is sent to the people, and no file is changed.

The person

First and last name. A middle name only if you know it.

The company

A web address, or just the company name. The skill looks the website up if needed.

1

Read the person and the company

Works on its own

Takes the name and the company from your message, for one person or a whole list, and tidies the company web address.

Result: a clean first name, last name and company domain, ready to use.

1a. What it needs

First name, last name and the company web address. A middle name is only used if you give one. The skill never asks for it.

1b. Tidy the web address

https://www.fielmann.eu/de becomes fielmann.eu. Prefixes, the "www" and everything after the first slash are removed.

1c. Only a company name given

The skill first looks up the company website on the web, then continues.

2

Work out the likely email addresses

Runs a script

A small program writes down every address the company could realistically have given this person, with the most common styles first.

Result: a ranked list of about 28 to 42 possible addresses.

Saves time: the most likely style is tested first.

2a. Common styles

For example first.last@company.com, f.last@company.com or firstlast@company.com. More styles are tried when a middle name is known.

2b. Names are made email-friendly

Accents and non-Latin letters become plain letters, titles such as Dr. and endings such as Jr. are dropped, and hyphens and apostrophes are removed.

3

Test each address with the mail checker

Asks the mail checker

Asks an outside mail-checking service whether the company's mail server would really accept each address. It works through the list one address at a time until it finds one that works.

Result: one confirmed address, or a clear reason why none could be confirmed.

Why it matters: a wrong address can bounce, harm our sender reputation or land in a spam trap.

3a. Careful pace

One address at a time, never in parallel, and at most 8 checks every 10 seconds (about one every 1.25 seconds). This keeps the service reliable.

3b. "Accepted"

The address is real and deliverable. The skill stops here for this person.

3c. "Catch-All"

The company accepts mail to any address, so this proves nothing about the person. It is noted as unconfirmed and the search goes on, because a real "Accepted" is more useful.

3d. Rejected or any other answer

The address is not valid. The skill moves to the next one.

3e. Mail server blocks the test

If two answers in a row are the same error, the server is blocking the checks themselves. The skill stops and reports Undeterminable: the mail server is blocking verification.

3f. No stopping point reached

All addresses on the list are tested before the skill concludes that nothing could be confirmed.

4

Report the result in the chat

Works on its own

Writes a short answer for each person. No file is created unless you ask for one.

Result: a plain answer: valid, unconfirmed, or best guess.

4a. Valid

The exact address or addresses that were accepted.

4b. Catch-All

Listed separately and clearly labelled unconfirmed.

4c. Everything else

Not listed one by one. Only a count, for example "24 other formats tested, all rejected".

4d. Nothing confirmed

The skill gives its best guess, the single most likely address, clearly marked as unverified.

5

Lists of people

Works on its own

If you give a list, steps 1 to 4 are repeated for each person in turn, and the results are grouped by person.

Result: one short result block per person.

5a. Same careful pace

People are also handled one after another, not at the same time, for the same reliability reasons.

End

Output: a short answer in the chat

You get one clear answer per person: a confirmed address, an unconfirmed one, or a best guess that is marked as unverified. No file is created unless you ask for one.
